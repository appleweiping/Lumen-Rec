Pointwise LLM recommenders ask a yes/no question about a user--item pair and use the probability of \emph{Yes} both to rank
candidates and as a confidence for abstaining, exposure control and data pruning. What does that confidence know? We audit it
for Qwen3-8B, zero-shot and LoRA-tuned (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct as a replication backbone), on rated panels whose labels match the
question and, zero-shot, on four same-candidate next-item panels (10k users, 101 candidates), in a pre-registered protocol with
non-LLM references on the same pairs and confirmation on untouched users. We split the log-odds into a user offset, an item prior
(the model's mean answer for the item under other users' histories) and a pair-specific residual, and measure how much the
residual adds, as a predictor of the label, to an item mean and to matrix factorisation.
Fine-tuned UAUC is below the item mean on all four rated panels (by 0.016 on ML-1M and, confirmed, by 0.08 to 0.13 on three Amazon
domains), the item prior explains 66--90\% of the within-user variance of the fine-tuned confidence, and the residual adds at most 0.010 UAUC to
the prior and the item mean: positive only on ML-1M with Qwen3-8B, and not replicated with Llama. Fine-tuning mostly teaches the
item: adapters trained on labels permuted within items or on item-mean pseudo-labels keep 85--92\% of the gain (Qwen3-8B).
