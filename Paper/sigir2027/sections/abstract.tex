Pointwise LLM recommenders ask a yes/no question about a user--item pair and use the probability of \emph{Yes} both to rank
candidates and as a confidence for abstaining, exposure control and data pruning. What does that confidence know? We report a
pre-registered audit of an 8B backbone, zero-shot and LoRA-tuned (plus a replication backbone), on rated panels whose labels
match the question and, zero-shot, on same-candidate next-item panels (four domains, 10k users, 101 candidates), with non-LLM
references and confirmation on untouched users. We decompose the confidence into a user offset, an item prior and personal
evidence, show that no strictly increasing per-user calibration map can change per-user AUC or within-user NDCG, and test the
remaining routes (item-dependent corrections, cross-user serving and exposure, data pruning) against their own controls.
