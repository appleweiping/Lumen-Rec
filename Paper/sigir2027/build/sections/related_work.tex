\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Pointwise yes/no scoring and its confidence.}
TALLRec~\citep{bao2023tallrec} tunes an LLM with LoRA to answer whether a user will like an item, CoLLM~\citep{zhang2025collm} and
BinLLM~\citep{zhang2024binllm} add collaborative information, and ReLLa~\citep{lin2024rella} retrieves behaviours and scores a pair by the
two-way Yes/No softmax, the sigmoid of the logit audited here. These models are judged by discrimination (AUC, UAUC, log loss), rarely
by reliability. IR rankers score relevance by the probability of a ``true'' token~\citep{nogueira2020document}; finer relevance grades help
zero-shot pointwise rankers~\citep{zhuang2024beyond}, and pairwise prompting avoids the calibration across prompts that pointwise scores
need~\citep{qin2024prp}. Generative recommenders~\citep{geng2022p5,liao2024llara,bao2025bigrec} face distorted sequence-level
confidence~\citep{bao2024d3}, which one Yes/No position avoids; zero-shot LLM rankers are swayed by popularity and candidate
position~\citep{hou2024llmrank}, and LLMs partly memorise MovieLens-1M~\citep{dipalma2025memorize}. At the output level, LLM
recommenders recover personal preference poorly under pointwise rating prompts once user rating bias and item quality are
controlled~\citep{tan2025perrecbench}, zero-shot LLMs trail collaborative filtering in rating prediction while fine-tuned ones approach
it~\citep{kang2023llmrating}, and they barely use the order of the history~\citep{kim2025lostinsequence}. None of these measures what the token
confidence itself encodes, how fine-tuning changes it, or which uses of it survive a matched control.

\paragraph{Uncertainty, calibration and answer priors.}
LLMs are overconfident when they verbalise confidence~\citep{xiong2024llmuncertainty} but fairly calibrated in token probabilities on
multiple-choice questions~\citep{kadavath2022language}; fine-tuning shapes confidence~\citep{kapoor2024taught,li2025conftuner}, and
calibration can depend on how close an input is to others~\citep{xiong2023procal}. Binary answers carry input-independent priors, removed by
contextual~\citep{zhao2021calibrate} or batch calibration~\citep{zhou2024batch} or PMI scoring~\citep{holtzman2021surface}; yes/no answers
carry order and wording biases that crossing a statement with its negation separates, leaving idiosyncratic per-item
residuals~\citep{huang2026yesno}, and LLMs prefer recognised brands~\citep{chu2026incumbent}. The two-view contrast evaluated here is the
log-odds analogue of the symmetrised inference rule of contrast-consistent search~\citep{burns2023ccs}. For LLM recommenders, UQRec~\citep{kweon2025uqrec}
decomposes list-wise uncertainty, GUIDER~\citep{xu2026guider} re-ranks by data and model uncertainty, UGR~\citep{fan2026ugr} uses confidence
tokens for rejection and truncation, EviRank~\citep{yan2026evirank} estimates position-level confidence, an audit scores verbalised
confidence by popularity stratum~\citep{ravikumar2026hallucinating}, KnowSA~\citep{lee2026knowsa} finds popularity and uncertainty poor proxies
of item knowledge, uncertainty-aware recommendation adapts to low-active users~\citep{zou2026uncertainty}, FACTER thresholds a fairness
score conformally~\citep{fayyazi2025facter}, and selective escalation to an LLM did not help in a sparse study~\citep{zhou2026routerec}.
Calibrating personalised ranking scores is older~\citep{kweon2022calibrated}. The decomposition below is the biased-MF baseline predictor
applied to log-odds, with the item term read off the model, and echoes the separation of interest from conformity in causal
embeddings~\citep{zheng2021dice}; a prior-offset LoRA is a product of experts with a bias-only expert~\citep{clark2019easyway}.

\paragraph{Popularity, exposure, pruning and serving.}
Popularity bias~\citep{abdollahpouri2017controlling} has causal treatments~\citep{wei2021macr,zhang2021pda} and is amplified by SFT and DPO
in LLM recommenders~\citep{gao2025flower,gao2025sprec}, whereas a prompted LLM recommender showed less of it than conventional
ones~\citep{lichtenberg2024popularity} and LLM recommenders can be unfair to items~\citep{jiang2024itemfairness}; in entity question
answering, LLM confidence rises with answer popularity~\citep{ni2026popular}. Feedback loops raise homogeneity and popularity
bias~\citep{chaney2018feedback,mansoury2020feedback}, also with LLM components~\citep{park2026echotrace}; we measure exposure statically.
LLM-recommender training data are selected by influence and effort~\citep{lin2024dealrec} or by coresets, where loss- and gradient-based scores
fall below random~\citep{mei2025goracs} (neither tests the uncertainty of a zero-shot model, so we compare with class-matched random
pruning); training dynamics and data pruning more generally~\citep{swayamdipta2020cartography,sorscher2022pruning}, uncertainty-aware
instruction selection~\citep{liu2024selectit} and claim-level cuts at an uncertainty quantile~\citep{wu2025unit} are related, and
recommenders are denoised by dropping large-loss interactions~\citep{wang2021tce} or by thresholding an LLM's generation
probability~\citep{wang2026llm4dsr}. Serving by a confidence score is a performance-prediction decision~\citep{bellogin2011predicting}
and selective classification~\citep{geifman2017selective}, which can widen group gaps~\citep{jones2021selective}. We test each use
against a matched control.
