Pointwise LLM recommenders ask a yes/no question about a user--item pair and use the probability of \emph{Yes} both to rank
candidates and as a confidence for abstaining, exposure control and data pruning. We ask what that confidence knows. We report
a pre-registered audit of an 8B backbone, zero-shot and LoRA-tuned (plus a replication backbone), on rated panels whose
labels match the question and, zero-shot, on full-scale same-candidate next-item panels (four domains, 10k users, 101
candidates). Rated tables carry non-LLM references; confirmation uses untouched users only. We decompose the confidence into
a user offset, an item prior and personal evidence, and show that per-user monotone calibration
cannot change per-user AUC or within-user NDCG. We test the routes that remain (item-dependent corrections, cross-user
serving and exposure, data pruning), each against its own control.
