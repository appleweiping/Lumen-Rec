\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper audits what the token confidence of yes/no LLM recommenders knows, with labels that match the question and a
protocol registered before the confirmatory data were scored. Two statements do not depend on the data: the confidence decomposes into
a user offset, an item prior and personal evidence, and no strictly increasing per-user map of the scores can change per-user
AUC or within-user NDCG (Section~\ref{sec:prelim}).

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Every claim is scoped to same-candidate evaluation with popularity-sampled negatives, which is not full-catalogue ranking
and can order systems differently from it.
% MISSING_REF: Krichene and Rendle, KDD 2020, sampled metrics (support for the last clause); see paper_missing_refs.txt
Rated labels exist only for explicit-feedback datasets and for users with enough likes and dislikes, and the untouched users
available for confirmation are limited by the data (\DATANEEDED{confirmation users per rated panel, from the split file}). Zero-shot
confidence depends on the prompt; we report the registered prompt and a single remedy round, not a prompt search. Exposure is
analysed statically: the registered fully observed validity check was not met in the zero-shot regime, so no feedback loop is
simulated and no claim about echo-chamber dynamics is made. Item text of public catalogues may have been seen in pre-training.
Only two 8B backbones (the second on \DATANEEDED{panels run}) and one LoRA configuration are covered, and the registration is
a dated document written by the team that ran the experiments, not a public-registry entry.
