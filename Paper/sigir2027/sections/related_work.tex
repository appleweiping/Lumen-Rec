\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Pointwise yes/no scoring.}
TALLRec~\citep{bao2023tallrec} tunes an LLM with LoRA to answer whether a user will like an item, CoLLM~\citep{zhang2025collm} and
BinLLM~\citep{zhang2024binllm} add collaborative information, and ReLLa~\citep{lin2024rella} retrieves behaviours and scores a pair
by the two-way Yes/No softmax, the sigmoid of the logit audited here, reporting AUC, log loss and accuracy. IR rankers score
relevance by the probability of a ``true'' token~\citep{nogueira2020document}; finer relevance grades help zero-shot pointwise
rankers~\citep{zhuang2024beyond}, and pairwise prompting avoids the calibration across prompts that pointwise scores
need~\citep{qin2024prp}. Generative recommenders~\citep{geng2022p5,liao2024llara,bao2025bigrec} face distorted sequence-level
confidence~\citep{bao2024d3}, which one Yes/No position avoids; zero-shot LLM rankers for recommendation are swayed by popularity and
candidate position~\citep{hou2024llmrank}, and LLMs partly memorise MovieLens-1M~\citep{dipalma2025memorize}.

\paragraph{Confidence, calibration and answer priors.}
LLMs are overconfident when they verbalise confidence~\citep{xiong2024llmuncertainty} but fairly calibrated in token probabilities on
multiple-choice questions~\citep{kadavath2022language}. Binary answers carry input-independent priors, removed by
contextual~\citep{zhao2021calibrate} or batch calibration~\citep{zhou2024batch} or PMI scoring~\citep{holtzman2021surface}; yes/no
answers show wording-driven bias with idiosyncratic per-item residuals~\citep{huang2026yesno}, LLMs prefer recognised
brands~\citep{chu2026incumbent}, and the two-view contrast \method{} belongs to contrast-consistent search~\citep{burns2023ccs}. For LLM
recommenders, UQRec~\citep{kweon2025uqrec} decomposes list-wise uncertainty, UGR~\citep{fan2026ugr} uses confidence tokens for
rejection and truncation, EviRank~\citep{yan2026evirank} estimates position-level confidence, an audit scores verbalised confidence by
popularity stratum~\citep{ravikumar2026hallucinating}, KnowSA~\citep{lee2026knowsa} finds popularity and uncertainty poor proxies of
item knowledge, FACTER thresholds a fairness score conformally~\citep{fayyazi2025facter}, and selective escalation to an LLM did not
help in a sparse study~\citep{zhou2026routerec}. None asks how much of the token confidence is a user-independent item prior.

\paragraph{Popularity, exposure, pruning and serving.}
Popularity bias~\citep{abdollahpouri2017controlling} has causal treatments~\citep{wei2021macr,zhang2021pda} and is amplified by SFT
and DPO in LLM recommenders~\citep{gao2025flower,gao2025sprec}, whereas a prompted LLM recommender showed less of it than conventional
ones~\citep{lichtenberg2024popularity}; in entity question answering, LLM confidence rises with answer popularity~\citep{ni2026popular}.
Feedback loops raise homogeneity and popularity bias~\citep{chaney2018feedback,mansoury2020feedback}, also with LLM
components~\citep{park2026echotrace}; we measure exposure statically. LLM-recommender training data are selected by influence and
effort~\citep{lin2024dealrec} or coresets~\citep{mei2025goracs}, instruction tuning prunes at an uncertainty quantile~\citep{wu2025unit},
and recommenders are denoised by dropping large-loss interactions~\citep{wang2021tce} or by thresholding an LLM's generation
probability~\citep{wang2026llm4dsr}. Serving by a confidence score is a performance-prediction decision~\citep{bellogin2011predicting}
and selective classification~\citep{geifman2017selective}, which can widen group gaps~\citep{jones2021selective}. We test each use
against a matched control.
