\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{LLM-based recommendation and yes/no scoring.}
\mbox{TALLRec}~\citep{bao2023tallrec} tunes an LLM with LoRA to answer whether a user will like an item, CoLLM~\citep{zhang2025collm}
and \mbox{BinLLM}~\citep{zhang2024binllm} add collaborative information and ReLLa~\citep{lin2024rella} retrieves behaviours; all
evaluate \py{} by AUC or UAUC, not by what it encodes. IR rankers likewise score relevance by the probability of a ``true''
token~\citep{nogueira2020document}. In generative LLM recommenders~\citep{geng2022p5,liao2024llara,kim2024allmrec,bao2025bigrec,chen2024sdpo}
ghost tokens and length normalisation distort sequence-level confidence~\citep{bao2024d3}, which one \texttt{Yes}/\texttt{No}
position avoids.
% METHOD-SLOT-BEGIN
Unlike CoLLM and BinLLM, the optional prior-offset LoRA adds a stop-gradient item-prior offset to the logit.
% METHOD-SLOT-END
We audit the pointwise family with matched labels and non-LLM references.

\paragraph{Confidence and calibration.}
LLMs are overconfident when they verbalise confidence~\citep{xiong2024llmuncertainty}, token probabilities are fairly calibrated on
multiple-choice questions~\citep{kadavath2022language}, verbalised confidence can be trained with a proper scoring
rule~\citep{li2025conftuner}, and low-density samples are more overconfident at matched confidence~\citep{xiong2023procal}; ECE
summarises miscalibration~\citep{naeini2015ece}, and temperature scaling~\citep{guo2017calibration} cannot reorder a user's candidates
(Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}). For LLM recommenders, UQRec~\citep{kweon2025uqrec} decomposes list-wise uncertainty,
UGR~\citep{fan2026ugr} uses confidence tokens for user rejection and item truncation, EviRank~\citep{yan2026evirank} estimates
position-level confidence, an audit scores verbalised confidence against catalogue membership by popularity
stratum~\citep{ravikumar2026hallucinating}, KnowSA~\citep{lee2026knowsa} finds popularity and uncertainty poor proxies of item
knowledge, and selective escalation to an LLM did not help in a sparse fixed-candidate study~\citep{zhou2026routerec}. We audit
token-level yes/no confidence under a registered protocol and test confident serving (selective
classification~\citep{geifman2017selective}, which can magnify group disparities~\citep{jones2021selective}) against random coverage.

\paragraph{Yes-bias, answer priors and brand effects.}
Binary answers carry input-independent biases, removed by contextual calibration~\citep{zhao2021calibrate}, domain-conditional
PMI~\citep{holtzman2021surface} or batch calibration~\citep{zhou2024batch}; yes/no studies report a lean towards
\emph{no}~\citep{braun2025acquiescence}, framing asymmetry~\citep{zhang2025yesharder} and wording-driven bias with idiosyncratic
per-item residuals~\citep{huang2026yesno}, and LLMs prefer recognised over fictional brands~\citep{chu2026incumbent}.
Contrast-consistent search combines a statement with its negation~\citep{burns2023ccs}; a two-view contrast of a question and its
mirror belongs to this family, is not claimed as new and enters only as a registered arm (\method{}) with a paraphrase placebo and an
ensemble null. We ask instead how much of a \emph{personalised} recommender's \py{} is a user-independent item prior.

\paragraph{Popularity, exposure and feedback loops.}
Popularity bias~\citep{abdollahpouri2017controlling} has causal treatments~\citep{wei2021macr,zhang2021pda} and is amplified by SFT
and DPO in LLM recommenders~\citep{gao2025flower,gao2025sprec}; calibrated recommendation matches a list's genre mix to the
user~\citep{steck2018calibrated}, whereas our probability calibration follows work on ranking scores under missing-not-at-random
feedback~\citep{kweon2022calibrated}. Feedback loops raise homogeneity and popularity bias~\citep{chaney2018feedback,mansoury2020feedback},
and LLM-agent simulators support filter-bubble studies~\citep{zhang2024agent4rec}. Randomly exposed test
sets~\citep{schnabel2016treatments,marlin2009collaborative} and the fully observed KuaiRec~\citep{gao2022kuairec} address
missing-not-at-random evaluation; as our zero-shot KuaiRec pilot missed its registered bar, we use explicit ratings whose threshold
matches the question and keep sampled-negative panels under same-candidate scope~\citep{krichene2020sampled}. We ask whether
confidence is popularity-linked and measure exposure statically on next-item panels of an existing benchmark protocol, where C-CRP, a
verbalised-probability pointwise reranker, and eight published LLM-enhanced recommenders are context only.

\paragraph{Data pruning for LLM-based recommendation.}
Data selection for LLM-recommender tuning uses influence and effort scores~\citep{lin2024dealrec} or optimal-transport coresets, where
gradient- and loss-based scores fall below random~\citep{mei2025goracs}; instruction tuning has cut claims the model is unfamiliar
with at an uncertainty quantile~\citep{wu2025unit}. We test whether a recommender's own uncertainty identifies noisy training pairs,
against matched-random pruning.
