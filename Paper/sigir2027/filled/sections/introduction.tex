\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used as \emph{pointwise} recommenders: asked ``Will this user like this item?
Answer Yes or No,'' the model's probability of \emph{Yes} is the ranking score~\citep{bao2023tallrec,zhang2025collm,zhang2024binllm}.
The same number is read as a \emph{confidence}: a system may abstain or truncate a list when it is
low~\citep{fan2026ugr,kweon2024perk}, what is shown feeds back into what is learnt, a loop known to amplify popularity
bias~\citep{chaney2018feedback,mansoury2020feedback}, and fine-tuning data can be pruned by uncertainty~\citep{wu2025unit} or
coreset scores~\citep{lin2024dealrec,mei2025goracs}. Whether the confidence deserves this reading is largely untested for the
yes/no family, which is evaluated by discrimination (AUC, per-user AUC, UAUC) and log loss~\citep{lin2024rella} rather than by
reliability (calibration error, selective risk); uncertainty work on LLM recommenders addresses list-wise
rankings~\citep{kweon2025uqrec,yan2026evirank}, generative recommenders~\citep{fan2026ugr} or verbalised
confidence~\citep{ravikumar2026hallucinating}. Output-level studies find that, under pointwise rating prompts, LLM recommenders
follow item quality and user rating bias more than personal preference~\citep{tan2025perrecbench}, that zero-shot LLMs trail
collaborative filtering in rating prediction while fine-tuned ones approach it~\citep{kang2023llmrating}, and that LLM recommenders
under-use the user's history~\citep{kim2025lostinsequence,zhang2024cft}; none has measured what the token confidence itself encodes,
how fine-tuning changes it, or which uses of it survive a matched control, on labels that match the question and against a
leakage-free item mean and a collaborative-filtering reference on the same pairs~\citep{ferraridacrema2019progress}.

Above all, it is open how much of $\py$ is a prior about the item and how much is evidence about this user. Write the log-odds
of \emph{Yes} as $\ell(u,i)=b_u+\pi(i)+e(u,i)$: a user offset, an item prior that the model brings to the item whoever asks
(possibly popularity-linked), and a pair-specific residual that carries whatever personal evidence there is. Only $\pi+e$ orders
a user's candidates, and the user offset is what per-user calibration cannot use: by an invariance lemma
(Section~\ref{sec:prelim}) any strictly increasing per-user map, such as temperature or Platt
scaling~\citep{guo2017calibration}, leaves per-user AUC and within-user NDCG unchanged, so outcomes change only through
item-dependent corrections, cross-user decisions and training. We measure personal information by prediction: the UAUC that the
residual adds to the prior and an item mean, beside what matrix factorisation adds.

We audit the confidence of yes/no recommenders through six practitioner questions, S1--S6, posed as research questions with
registered endpoints (Table~\ref{tab:rq}): is the model sure when it is right (RQ1) and unsure when it is wrong (RQ2); does
serving the most confident items over-expose head items (RQ3, static); how well does confidence track labels that match the
question, and how much of it is item prior (RQ4); is it higher for popular items, and is that warranted once quality is
controlled (RQ5); and does pruning the training examples that the zero-shot model is most uncertain about help more than matched
random pruning (RQ6)?

\paragraph{A registered audit.}
Panels, endpoints, thresholds and a claim-admission rule were registered before any confirmatory result was analysed
(Section~\ref{sec:protocol}): a statement beyond one panel needs confirmation on untouched users and the same sign, with its own
interval excluding zero, on a fresh Amazon domain and on a second backbone. Not every gate was met: under the registered zero-shot
prompt a pilot on 1,500 ML-1M users reached UAUC 0.587 against a bar of 0.60 (the next-item component was met: NDCG@10 0.2093
against 0.1863), and on the fully observed KuaiRec matrix~\citep{gao2022kuairec} the zero-shot UAUC was 0.469 against 0.58, so
exposure is analysed statically. One registered prompt-remedy round (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}) selected, on development users,
the label-aligned prompt that asks whether the user would rate the item 4 stars or higher; on 1,683
untouched ML-1M users it reached UAUC 0.603 (95\% CI 0.594--0.612) against 0.576 for the registered
prompt, so the bar (a point estimate) was met. As TALLRec-style recommenders are fine-tuned~\citep{bao2023tallrec}, the LoRA regime
was to carry the main results if it met its own registered gate, a mean UAUC of at least 0.65 over three seeds. It did (mean
0.742; seeds 0.740, 0.745, 0.743), so the fine-tuned regime is the
main object and the zero-shot regime its contrast.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{list}{}{\introlistsetup}
\item[C1] \emph{What the confidence encodes.} A decomposition of the yes/no log-odds into user offset, item prior and pair-specific
  residual, with the information gain over the prior-only item mean as the measure of personal information, benchmarked against an
  item mean and matrix factorisation on the same pairs; a within-user estimator and the complementarity to collaborative filtering;
  and three controls for what fine-tuning teaches: within-item label permutation, an item-only teacher and a store-name knockout
  with a real-brand placebo.
\item[C2] \emph{Which uses of the confidence survive a control.} The invariance lemma and a controlled test of each route it
  leaves open: item-dependent corrections (including a two-view contrast~\citep{burns2023ccs} that we evaluate, not propose)
  against a paraphrase placebo and an ensemble null; selective serving~\citep{geifman2017selective} against random coverage;
  static exposure against the pool and eight published recommenders; uncertainty-selected pruning against class-matched random
  pruning over five seeds.
\item[C3] \emph{A pre-registered, hash-chained protocol.} Qwen3-8B, zero-shot and with LoRA (three seeds), replicated with
  Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, on rated panels with matched labels (ML-1M; Amazon Toys, Video Games, Sports) and, zero-shot, on
  same-candidate next-item panels of an earlier benchmark protocol~\citep{anon2026benchmark} (four domains $\times$ 10k users
  $\times$ 101 candidates), with non-LLM references, user-clustered intervals, Holm correction within registered families, fresh-user
  confirmation and a table of deviations (Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}).
\end{list}

\paragraph{Findings in brief.}
\emph{(i) What the confidence tracks (Section~\ref{sec:exp-tracks}).} On the four rated panels (ML-1M, Toys, Video Games, Sports) the
fine-tuned Qwen3-8B reaches UAUC 0.742, 0.558, 0.580 and 0.534, against 0.759, 0.684, 0.662 and 0.624 for the item mean on the same
rows and 0.791, 0.539, 0.532 and 0.534 for matrix factorisation. It is below the item mean on all four panels, and Holm-confirmed
below on the three Amazon panels ($-0.126$, $-0.082$, $-0.090$); on ML-1M the gap is $-0.016$ (exploratory), and Llama-3.1-8B
matches the item mean there (0.760) and stays $0.123$ below it on Toys. The item prior explains 0.90, 0.66, 0.72 and 0.74 of the
within-user variance of the fine-tuned confidence (zero-shot: 0.39 on ML-1M and 0.33 on Sports; the other two panels fall below the
reliability bar). The pair-specific residual adds at most $+0.010$ UAUC to the prior and the item mean (ML-1M, interval
$[0.003,0.017]$; at most $+0.002$ on the other panels, where the zero-shot Sports value, $-0.009$, is confirmed negative), against
$+0.036$ for the MF residual on ML-1M; that supervision raises this gain (P1) holds on ML-1M ($+0.011$, $p=0.004$, three of
three seeds) and finds no evidence on Llama-3.1-8B ($+0.004$, $p=0.38$).
\emph{(ii) What fine-tuning teaches (Section~\ref{sec:exp-teach}).} An adapter trained on labels permuted within each item keeps
$R=0.92$ of the gain from real labels on ML-1M, and one trained on pseudo-labels from the item mean alone keeps $0.85$ on ML-1M
and, with a wide interval, $1.3$ on Toys (both read ITEM\_DRIVEN under the registered rule; the teacher adapters reach UAUC 0.720
and 0.564--0.571 against 0.742 and 0.558 for the real-label adapters). The fine-tuned LLM adds nothing detectable to matrix
factorisation ($\mathcal G_{\rm LLM|CF}\le0.005$, confirmed on none of the three Amazon panels), and the store-name knockout is
INDETERMINATE on every panel and model, so the confidence is called popularity-linked, not familiarity-linked.
