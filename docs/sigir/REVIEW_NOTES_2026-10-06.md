## R-B. Positioning and novelty review (lens: uncertainty-aware LLM4Rec literature)

Estimated acceptance probability about 20% (15-30%); about 30-35% if a controlled, replicated positive use (D1/D2 below) succeeds; 10-15% if every route is null. The most novel claim: the item-only TRAINING controls (FT-C R = 0.92, FT-Q R = 0.85) with G_LLM|CF <= 0.005: a causal statement about what TALLRec-style SFT learns.
The claim most likely to be dismissed as known: 'LLM recommenders follow item quality/popularity' (PerRecBench, rating-prediction studies, Zero-Shot Rankers, Flower/SPRec). Sharpened contribution statement (adopted as the basis of the abstract and introduction): 'With training controls that remove all user-item evidence, we show that LoRA-tuned Yes/No LLM recommenders
learn item-level label rates rather than personal preference ... Since no per-user recalibration can reorder a user's candidates, we test the decisions where such confidence can still act (cross-user serving, exposure, item-dependent corrections, data selection) each against a matched control, and find [X].'

Missing or uncited works (all verified to exist by the reviewer; to be verified again before they enter references.bib): GUIDER (AAAI 2026); Disentangling User Interest and Conformity for Recommendation with Causal Embedding (WWW 2021; the decomposition is the biased-MF baseline predictor applied to log-odds: say so); 'Large Language Models Must Be Taught to Know What They Don't Know' (NeurIPS 2024) and ConfTuner (NeurIPS 2025) (both in the bib, uncited);
Clark et al., 'Don't Take the Easy Way Out' (EMNLP 2019: the prior-offset LoRA is a product of experts with a bias-only expert); Dataset Cartography (EMNLP 2020), 'Beyond neural scaling laws' (NeurIPS 2022), SelectIT (NeurIPS 2024) for S6; 'Item-side Fairness of LLM-based Recommendation System' (WWW 2024) for S3; 'Uncertainty-Calibrated Recommendations for Low-Active Users' (KDD 2026); kweon2022calibrated (in the bib, uncited).
Misdescriptions to fix: MIRROR/CCS (CCS is an unsupervised probe on hidden states; say 'the log-odds analogue of CCS's symmetrised inference rule'); UNIT (it deletes claims inside responses for truthfulness; P2 deletes whole examples: drop 'in the spirit of UNIT'); Lost in Sequence (order sensitivity, not 'under-use of the history'); GORACS (state its finding that loss/gradient scores fall below random); 'None asks how much of the confidence is a user-independent item prior' holds at the logit level only.
Constructive directions (D1 prior-aware group-conditional serving; D2 prior-cancelling LoRA with within-item pairs and a within-item-permutation falsification arm; D3 prior-corrected exposure; D4 decomposition-informed pruning) cost 130-210 of the 600 GPU-hours. Gaps against strong papers of this kind: one uncertainty estimator (a seed-ensemble disagreement estimate is free from the three adapters), two 8B backbones, one recipe, power (366 / 943 / 820 / 997 TEST users),
no pruning baselines (DEALRec, GORACS, GraNd/EL2N), no figures, unreadable shrunk tables, text before the references over the limit.

## R-C. Methodology and statistics review (lens: threats to validity; recomputed from the stored files)

No fatal flaw; eight major and three minor findings; ten things verified as fine (SHA-1 chain of Amendments 1-3 and addenda 1-12 and revised 13 matches the pilot log; about 25 table cells match; tie-aware per-user AUC, user-cluster bootstrap, Holm code, cross-fitting without within-user leakage;
TRAIN/EVAL disjoint, T_d equal to Gate-FT's; item-prior share + e-share sum to 1.000-1.031; G6 0.6033 [0.5944, 0.6124]; E1; Sports quarantine; temperatures on VALID only; Platt invariance max |dAUC| 0.000).
1. Abstract/introduction/conclusion break the paper's own admission and direction-word rules (the registered second-backbone replication panel of the ML-1M claims is Llama on ML-1M, where the fine-tuned E-J is +0.001 [-0.012, 0.014]; variance shares, R and R_Q belong to no Holm family and cannot be 'confirmed'; the ML-1M E-J is exploratory; '85-92%' omits Toys R_Q = 1.34; of 31 registered rated tests 12 are confirmed and only E-B meets the registered admission rule). Fix: wording.
2. The item mean is not information-matched: q-hat uses ratings up to t and the whole corpus (Toys 16.3M, Sports 19.6M events) while the LoRA trains on 15-23k labels; against the time-matched mean q-hat_T the fine-tuned contrasts are ML-1M -0.014 [-0.027, -0.001], Toys -0.075, Video_Games +0.000 [-0.026, 0.026] (p 0.99), Sports -0.081; 'information-matched' is a misnomer (time-matched only); the MF
   fold-in uses the whole earlier history (median 80 events on ML-1M) while the prompt shows 10, so G = 0.010 against G_CF = 0.036 is not history-matched either. Fix: print the q-hat_T contrasts with intervals, add the TRAIN-label item mean and a 10-event MF (addendum 15).
3. The MF reference is broken on the three Amazon panels (training RMSE 0.10-0.11, held-out RMSE 1.9-2.0; 38/54/26% of TEST pairs cold and scored as ties; item-bias-only UAUC 0.679/0.645/0.607 on warm pairs against 0.571/0.572/0.548 for the full MF; Toys verified by me from `references/cf_block/mf_temporal`). So 'no personal signal on the Amazon panels' and the Amazon G_CF and E-F are not
   established. Fix: tune the regularisation on CAL, report the item-bias-only model, recompute G_CF and E-F (addendum 15); until then write 'no positive control on the Amazon panels'.
4. The registered E-D estimator is biased and the paper headlines its artefacts (Sports zero-shot -0.0095 by E-D, +0.0016 by E-W; Video_Games fine-tuned -0.0091 against -0.0015); the Sports P1 (+0.0118) exists only because G_ZS is biased; the E-D Holm family has 2 members per panel (a Holm over the 8 G tests confirms only ML-1M fine-tuned, p_holm 0.032; over all 16 E-D tests none survives);
   the stackers are fitted once on one 2-fold split and the bootstrap holds the coefficients fixed. Fix: wording ('estimator-dependent'; cross-panel counts unadjusted) and a full-refit bootstrap (addendum 14).
5. G is not a clean measure of personal information: M2 = [q-hat, pi-hat, e-hat] spans [q-hat, pi-hat, l], so l acts as a ninth noisy draw of the item prior and G can be positive from denoising pi-hat alone when r_8 < 1; upper 95% bounds 0.017 (ML-1M) and up to 0.011 on the Amazon panels; minimum detectable effects 0.008-0.014 (E-D), 0.002-0.012 (E-W), 0.012-0.016 (P1).
   Fix: wording with bounds and MDE, and a donor-placebo analysis (addendum 14 item 3).
6. FT-C and FT-Q do not show what fine-tuning teaches: the FT-Q teacher uses full-corpus q-hat (incl. EVAL users' pre-T_d ratings the real adapter never had), agrees with the real labels only 72/69/66/66% (kappa about 0.37/0.27/0.24/0.20) and on Toys the teacher adapters beat the real ones (0.564/0.571 vs 0.559/0.556): distillation of a stronger target, not retention; R/R_Q intervals are fragile (Toys R_Q printed
   '1.335 +- 1.186' but stored [0.674, 3.046]; the E-B denominator's interval is [0.006, 0.056]; a Fieller interval is needed); no control removes a readout/answer-format shift; two seeds; the 0.5 cut-off was set when R about 0.93 was foreseeable; `summary.json` sets ft_wording complete = true although the Video_Games and Sports FT-Q runs have not run (do not use it until they have);
   the lower bound of R_Q must exceed 0.5 on Video_Games and Sports (denominators [0.037, 0.088], [0.015, 0.069]). Fix: report UAUC(real) - UAUC(control) (ML-1M real-minus-permuted 0.009 [0.001, 0.017], about the size of G_FT), print [lo, hi], Fieller intervals (addendum 14 item 5), and a global-permutation readout control (GPU, to be registered).
7. The sampled-candidate design confounds the next-item serving and exposure endpoints: under random serving niche users already score NDCG@10 0.335 against 0.201 for mainstream users (Toys), so p_max's higher served share for niche users reflects how easy their events are; the 'quintiles' hold 17/23/13/47/0% of users; max_logit gains 0.001, p_max 0.103 (the top candidate standing out in the list, which popularity-sampled
   negatives can create); Delta_head is 0.059 against the pool but 0.040 against the target; with n = 10,000 trivial effects are 'significant' (S5 head-minus-tail mean p -0.0024 [-0.0031, -0.0017]); the paper calls the Brier score the Brier of p_max against top-1 correctness while the code computes the multiclass Brier; 69% of events fall in the first ECE bin. Fix: stratified diagnostics, a smallest effect size of interest, wording (to be registered with the serving-control follow-up).
8. The disclosure of later registration documents is stale (appendix lists addenda 1-8 only; addenda 9, 11, 12, 13 and the 2026-10-06 wording change are missing; DEVIATIONS.md rows 21-22 were added on 2026-10-06). 'Hash-chained' overstates (entries do not commit to earlier entries; the timing rests on author-controlled pushes), 'pre-registered' needs qualifying. Suggested wording: 'Each analysis was registered in dated internal amendments whose SHA-1 hashes were logged before it
   ran (Amendment 1 retroactively). The audit design followed a failed pilot. Endpoints marked dagger and all addenda after addendum 8 were added after partial results and are exploratory where those results had been seen. Abstract claims are limited to registered tests that meet the registered rule; descriptive estimates are labelled.' Depositing OpenTimestamps proofs with the anonymous artefact would make the timing checkable (to offer to the user).
9-11 (minor): 'popularity-linked' is forced by a registered default (knockout rule needs an absolute placebo of at most 0.05; Toys zero-shot pseudonym 0.40 vs placebo 0.14); the r_8 rule and the donor design are applied unevenly (e-share printed for r_8 < 0.7; per-item donors; the user-only bootstrap ignores item and donor variance on ML-1M where 97% of items repeat); the sparse-row G_prior is positive in all four runs (+0.014/+0.019/+0.013/+0.025;
   pooled +0.014, SE 0.006, exploratory) but '0 of 2' hides it; S6 has a minimum detectable effect of about 0.012 UAUC; 'EVAL users never trained on' is not literally true for FT-Q and the method slot (they train on item means that include CAL ratings; not TEST leakage).
Five analyses to add first (R-C): donor-placebo G; matched references (q-hat_T contrasts with intervals, TRAIN-label item mean, tuned MF, item-bias-only, 10-event fold-in; recompute G_CF and E-F); honest uncertainty (full-refit bootstrap, user x item bootstrap, cross-panel Holm, MDE table, Fieller R); readout control (global-permutation adapter, about 8 GPU-h, plus R for FT-N); next-item artefact checks (AUROC of p_max and serving gain within strata of the positive item's popularity group and niche quintiles; Delta_head against the target; a SESOI).
